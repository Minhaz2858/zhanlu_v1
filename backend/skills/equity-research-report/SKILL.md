# Equity Research Report

Create institutional-grade investment research with sell-side styling and comprehensive section structure. Benchmarked against Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and J.P. Morgan research styles.

## When to use

- Equity / fixed income / strategy / sector / derivative / ETF / quantitative research requests
- "Write an investment research report", "initiate coverage", "sell-side report", "research note"
- Any request needing a professional research document with exhibits and disclosures

## Report structure (use exactly)

1. **Cover page** — company/asset name, ticker, rating, target price, current price, market cap, report date, analyst attribution, "Equity Research" banner
2. **Executive Summary / Investment Thesis** — 3-5 key bullets: rating, target, catalysts, risks
3. **Key Statistics table** — price, shares, market cap, EV, P/E, EV/EBITDA, dividend yield, 52-week range
4. **Rating & Valuation** — methodology (DCF / comps / SOTP), target price derivation, scenario analysis (bull/base/bear)
5. **Industry & Market Context** — market size, growth, competitive landscape, regulatory environment
6. **Company Analysis** — business model, products/services, revenue breakdown, margins, unit economics
7. **Financial Analysis** — historicals + forecasts: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, key ratios (use real data tables)
8. **Exhibits** — numbered exhibits (E1, E2, ...) for every chart/table referenced in text
9. **Risks** — company-specific and macro risks, each with likelihood/impact
10. **Glossary** — every technical term used
11. **Disclosures & Disclaimer** — analyst certification, rating definitions, distribution of ratings, regulatory disclosures

## Formatting rules (sell-side style)

- Dense information layout: tables and exhibits carry most of the content; prose is terse
- Every figure in text is cross-referenced to an exhibit: "Revenue grew 12% YoY (Exhibit 4)"
- Numbered exhibits with titles and source lines
- Consistent number formatting (millions, decimal places)
- Rating scale: Buy / Hold / Sell with clear definitions
- Target price always has a valuation basis, never a bare number

## Data grounding

- Ground every figure in real data from the connected datasource or published filings
- Never invent financials; mark estimates as estimates with methodology
- For fake-data/simulation requests, label all figures "illustrative" and say so on the cover

## Output

- DOCX/PDF default for full reports; PPTX for an investor summary deck
- Keep the same numbering and cross-references across formats
